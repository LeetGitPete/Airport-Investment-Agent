# Key Tradeoffs

Every constraint we hit and every choice we made, with its impact and what we did about it. This is a compressed
view of the living log `docs/design/known-limitations-and-tradeoffs.md` — the row numbers are that log's ids, and
no row is omitted. **Constraint** = imposed by the data or the one-day budget; **Decision** = our choice, and we
own it.

Two reading notes. (1) The log was assembled from four parallel worktrees and renumbered at merge, so a handful of
its rows still point at pre-merge row numbers (row 1 cites "row 22" for the OTP cut, which is now row 28; rows 5,
7 and 14 cite "rows 24/26", now rows 40 and 42). The prose is right; only the pointers are stale. (2) The scope
cut that shapes most of this document is referred to as the "RESCOPE" and is dated 2026-08-16 in the log and in
code comments, while the proposal document `docs/superpowers/RESCOPE-2026-08-15.md` carries the previous day's
date — same event, decided late on the 15th and applied on the 16th.

## Constraints

| # | What | Impact | Mitigation / outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | BTS OTP raw detail (taxi-out, per-flight distance groups) is ~270 MB per month; 10 years of raw is infeasible in a day | Delay *detail* has no long history | Originally mitigated by the BTS Delay Cause dataset (2003->); the RESCOPE cut both that source and the OTP window (to 12 months, row 28), so no 10y delay-detail mitigation exists and `nas_delay_share` is absent at every horizon |
| 2 | The T-100 *International* Segment table code could not be found | No route-level international detail | A 15-minute probe of 15 candidate table codes all redirected to the TranStats homepage. `routes_month` is domestic-only (`is_international=False` on every row); Socrata's airport-level inbound/outbound international totals are the only international signal, and they are what `intl_pax_share` uses |
| 3 | FAA ASPM/OPSNET (official capacity and throughput) is login-walled | No official hourly capacity-vs-demand series | Congestion is *inferred* from delay, taxi-out, load factor and upgauging, plus curated declared capacities — and every answer says it is an inference |
| 4 | Per-airport monthly totals do not exist in Socrata before 2014 | The 10y horizon is 2016->2026; deeper history is annual only | FAA TAF actuals (1976->) back the long-run enplanement and operations series |
| 5 | Terminal and gate counts are in no free dataset | Gate-intensity metrics cannot be computed directly | The planned curated-YAML fallback did not work either: no authoritative public gate count was secured for *any* airport, so `pax_per_gate` and `deps_per_gate_day` are absent everywhere (row 42) |
| 6 | Forward airline schedules (OAG, Cirium) are paid products | No forward capacity-*supply* signal | FAA TAF forecast is used as the demand-side substitute (`taf_cagr_10y`, `taf_vs_actual_gap`) |
| 8 | Causal "why" (runway geometry, ATC procedures, politics) is not structured anywhere | Explanations need prose that no table contains | `data/curated/airport_facts.yaml`, every fact with its own source URL and as-of date; the LLM must cite it or say "unknown" |
| 9 | Free-tier LLM limits (~10 requests/minute on Gemini) | Tool loops must be short; bursts hit HTTP 429 | Budget of <=6 LLM calls per answer, follow-ups reuse session memory instead of re-dispatching, and **no silent fallback** — a provider failure is a loud, actionable error |
| 14 | O&D vs connecting share needs BTS DB1B/OD-40 (large quarterly files) | P3 lacks the market-quality signal rating agencies weight most | Cut by the RESCOPE before the timeboxed adapter attempt ran; `od_share` is absent at every horizon and presets renormalize over the remaining P3 metrics |
| 19 | Five tier-C metrics (`asv_utilization`, `terminal_sqft_per_nbeg`, `dscr`, `days_cash`, `use_agreement_type`) have no public source | P2 lacks capacity-utilization depth; P5 lacks debt-coverage depth | Kept in the registry as documented gaps: never scored, never invented, surfaced as "not available" with the reason when asked |
| 22 | OurAirports ICAO resolution falls back `icao_code` -> `ident` -> `gps_code` | A minority of rows can carry an identifier that disagrees with the true ICAO code | Fallback chain documented in the adapter; identity fields are display-only and all joins use IATA / FAA LocID |
| 23 | `hub_size`, `faa_region` and `commercial` arrive as placeholders at OurAirports ingest | Airports absent from TAF and Socrata keep placeholder identity values | Overwrite order is fixed in the snapshot build. *As implemented*, hub class and FAA region are overwritten by the TAF enrichment pass, while `airports.commercial` deliberately stays at its `False` placeholder: "commercial" is evaluated at query time from positive enplanements in Socrata or TAF (`data/commercial.py`) so it cannot drift from the tables that define it |
| 27 | `PRESET_NAMES` in the agent planner duplicates the preset list in `config/scoring_presets.yaml` | Adding a preset means touching two files, or the planner rejects it | Logged here, and a golden test runs every preset name end to end so the duplication cannot silently rot |
| 42 | Airport gate counts have no authoritative public source and were not secured for any airport | `pax_per_gate` and `deps_per_gate_day` (tier B, P2) are absent everywhere, despite being declared in the registry and up-weighted 3.0x in `terminal_expansion` | None available. Documented as a gap; a curated airport that has VMC/IMC and slot inputs but no gate count shows this pair as None without a dedicated note — a narrower gap than the general "no curated capacity inputs" note communicates |
| 43 | The T-100 backfill window (24 months) is shorter than a full 5 years | `spill_proxy` (3y) and `carrier_hhi` / `top_carrier_share` / `longhaul_dep_share` / `route_count_nonstop` (5y) are computed from fewer months than the horizon nominally implies | Every such value carries a `partial_window` quality flag stating exactly how many of the nominal months were available. The number is real; it is never fabricated or silently mislabelled |

## Decisions

| # | What | Impact | Mitigation / outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | FAA CATS Form 127 (airport financials) was verified as queryable in research, then cut in build | `cpe_usd` and `nonaero_rev_per_enpl` are absent at every horizon | Cut by the RESCOPE before an adapter was written (time budget). `derived/p5_finance.py` returns zero rows for both, with the reason recorded in `MISSING_REASONS` |
| 10 | Commit the DuckDB snapshot (~33 MB) rather than fetching data at first run | The reviewer runs offline and instantly; the data ages in place | `refresh` CLI plus `refresh --check` staleness per source cadence; vintages and periods shown in the UI sidebar and in answers |
| 11 | In-process Python tools rather than MCP as the primary transport | Simpler and provider-agnostic; not usable by an external MCP client as shipped | The `ToolRegistry` is transport-neutral; a FastMCP wrapper over the same registry is a small addition |
| 12 | One Concierge with structured dispatch to code and to LLM specialists, instead of free conversational multi-agent handoffs | Much lower variance and a bounded call budget; less emergent "agentic" behaviour | Dispatch is a validated `AnalysisRequest`; the only free-text channel is a hint capped at 200 characters (600 for `general_analyst`) |
| 13 | Ship a throwaway-account Gemini key in `.env` inside the zip | Zero setup for the reviewer; the key is live in a delivered artefact | Throwaway account, revoked after review; `.env` is gitignored, a commit-guard hook blocks staging it, and a missing or invalid key produces a clear error |
| 15 | No silent LLM degradation | If the provider fails, no answer is produced at all | Loud actionable error naming provider and status; the provider chain is the only resilience. The deterministic engine stays callable from code/CLI but is never presented as a user-facing fallback answer |
| 16 | A single LLM provider (Gemini free tier); Groq/NIM fail-over deferred | A 429 or outage means a loud error instead of a fail-over | The LiteLLM router already supports fallbacks: adding one is an entry in `config/providers.yaml` plus a key |
| 17 | `contracts/registry.py` reads `config/metrics.yaml` from disk — the one sanctioned I/O inside `contracts/` | Bends the "contracts are pure types" rule in one narrow place | Read-only, no network, path injectable via `load_registry(path)`; every other contracts module stays types and Protocols only. The payoff is that the registry loads without a data layer |
| 18 | Specialist hint capped at 200 characters (600 for `general_analyst`) | The Concierge cannot smuggle analysis through free text; long user nuance may be truncated | The structured `AnalysisRequest` fields carry the substance; hint is steering only, validated in contracts, and truncation is flagged in the tool trace |
| 20 | Freeze `contracts/` and `config/metrics.yaml` at tag `contracts-v1` before parallel work | Four workstreams built against a stable ABI; late modelling mistakes became expensive | Changing them requires a human decision, `CONTRACTS_UNFROZEN=1` and a rebase of every open worktree |
| 21 | `FeatureMatrix` never relabels a metric across horizons | Matrices at 5y/10y are sparser than at 12m | The rule is in the `get_feature_matrix` docstring and enforced by the shared contract suite; the scorer handles None and reports coverage per answer |
| 24 | Row vintage is the newest raw-file mtime, not the publisher's release date | A cached download reused months later carries its own file date, so vintages can understate data age | `file_vintage` always derives from the files themselves (never "now"), and `refresh --check` reports staleness against each source's cadence |
| 25 | `coverage` appears on analysis-tool results only (mean `ScoreRow.coverage`), not on plain data-tool results | The LLM cannot cite a coverage figure for a raw lookup | Design 03 amended accordingly; data tools still carry provenance and a `truncated` flag. Coverage is a property of a scored row set, not of a lookup |
| 26 | `low_confidence` ignores pillars that are absent by design for the preset | An answer scored on fewer pillars is not flagged low-confidence merely for that absence | The caveat text states the forgone weight explicitly ("Pillars P4, P5 not scored; forgone preset weight 0.20") |
| 28 | Ingest OTP for a trailing 12 months only (RESCOPE; supersedes the planned 36) | `pct_arr_delay_gt15` and `avg_dep_delay_min` are None at 3y/5y — only 12m is honest | `otp_months: 12` in `config/sources.yaml`; ~12 x 30 MB downloads instead of ~1.1 GB |
| 29 | Census population and Gazetteer centroids only, no BEA (RESCOPE) | `msa_gdp_per_capita` and `msa_gdp_cagr_5y` are None; `catchment.gdp_real_usd` is always None | `cbsa_population` and `cbsa_pop_cagr_5y` still land, so P4 keeps market size and growth; presets renormalize |
| 30 | Percentiles are computed against the whole airport universe (<=600 airports) partitioned by peer group, not only the airports named in the question | A regional question still ranks each airport against its national hub-class peers; a lone large hub is not compared to small hubs | Stated in every report's caveats ("Percentiles computed within ... across N airports"), and the matrix is fetched in one call |
| 31 | Long-haul passenger share weights departures on rows with seats > 0; the freight share weights freight pounds | A mixed passenger/cargo route counts fully as passenger departures | A convention flag rides on the metric, and the freight variant is what answers cargo questions such as ANC |
| 32 | Unmet-demand "signals" are quartile heuristics (percentile >= 0.75 within hub class) over the diagnose bundle | Indicative, not causal | Every signal is listed with a tick or cross and its value; the caveat says the signals "indicate, not prove" |
| 33 | `Plan.filters` carries the AnalysisRequest fields plus per-tool `tool_calls[{tool, args_json}]`, with arguments as a JSON string | Slightly clunkier schema; args are parsed by code | Keeps the Plan a single portable structured-output call with no nested free-form objects. Args are still pydantic-validated by the ToolRegistry, and bad JSON becomes a visible tool error, never a silent empty call |
| 34 | Specialist evidence is resolved by code from tool results via `evidence_refs[{iata, metric_id}]`; unresolved refs are dropped and listed in caveats | The specialist cannot introduce a number; a lazy specialist cites less than it could | The prompt requires the refs, matching falls back to metric id, and an ambiguous match is flagged in the caveats |
| 35 | If synthesis JSON fails to parse, fall back to the deterministic explanation as the headline with the note "synthesis text unavailable - showing raw report" | Rare, and visible to the user when it happens | Formatting degrades, reasoning does not: tables, citations and assumptions are unchanged. Provider failures still raise loudly |
| 36 | Specialist system prompts run 2-3k tokens because each metric slice carries full definitions, formulas, tiers, sources and caveats | More prompt tokens per specialist call than a summary slice would need | Accepted deliberately: the full slice is what stops the model inventing metric ids or definitions. A `metric_detail: summary` front-matter option is the fallback if the prompt budget ever bites |
| 37 | The three structured-output schemas are portable JSON-schema subsets (type/properties/required/items/enum/description only), using "none" / [] / "" as unset sentinels | No optional or union-typed fields; every key is required and the code maps sentinels back to None | Works unchanged on Gemini and on any provider added later; a test asserts the forbidden keywords (`anyOf`, `oneOf`, `$ref`, `additionalProperties`, `nullable`) never appear |
| 38 | The UI obtains the App through `AIRPORT_AGENT_APP_FACTORY="module:callable"` when set, else `build_app()` | One test-only environment hook in production code | Documented in `ui/bootstrap.py`; ignored when unset |
| 39 | The UI re-renders each live turn from history after `st.rerun()` instead of rendering it inline | One extra script rerun before the final answer appears | A single render path for every turn, first or not — which is what makes the Streamlit `AppTest` smoke tests meaningful. The transient plan preview is replaced by `Answer.plan_line` |
| 40 | Delay Cause, FAA CATS Form 127 and FAA AIP were all cut by the RESCOPE before any adapter was built | `nas_delay_share`, `cpe_usd`, `nonaero_rev_per_enpl` and `aip_per_enpl_10y` are absent everywhere; **P5 effectively reduces to `npias_dev_per_enpl`** | Each id keeps a registry entry and a derived function that returns zero rows with a reason in `MISSING_REASONS`; the frozen contract suite already permits these ids to be absent from a real implementation; presets renormalize over what landed |
| 41 | Declared-capacity convention: `declared_rate_vmc` = midpoint of the *best* visual configuration, `declared_rate_imc` = midpoint of the *worst* instrument configuration (excluding "Low Instrument") | `imc_capacity_ratio` measures adverse-weather capacity loss, not an average-case ratio; `peak_hour_ops_ratio` compares peak demand to the most optimistic throughput, so it reads as a *lower bound* on constraint | The convention is stated in `adapters/curated.py` and carried on every affected metric row. Independent second caveat: the underlying FAA Airport Capacity Profiles are 2014-2019 and reflect no runway or taxiway changes since |
| 44 | `refresh` probes backwards from the current month for the latest month that downloads, per source, instead of assuming a fixed publication lag | A source that is down or unusually delayed could land an older "latest" month than expected | The probe budget is capped (`_PROBE_SLACK_MONTHS`), and `--check` and the stored vintages always show the period actually ingested, never a guessed one |
| 45 | Per-row `vintage` for file-based sources is the raw file's mtime — when we downloaded it, not when the publisher released it (TAF is the exception: zip entry dates give the FAA's real 2026-03-20 publication) | Vintage reads fresher than the data is: NPIAS shows 2026-08-15 for a plan published 2024-09-30. Vintage is an upper bound on freshness, not a publication date | Every metric and `SourceVintage` also carries `period_start`/`period_end` taken from the data itself (NPIAS 2025..2029, Socrata 2014-01..2026-04, OTP 2025-07..2026-06); answers cite the period, which is honest. `refresh --check` measures staleness from `fetched_at` + cadence, never from vintage |
| 46 | `airports.hub_size` and `airports.faa_region` are updated in place from FAA TAF but keep `source_id = 'ourairports'` | Row-level provenance for those two columns understates their true source | The store is deliberately one-source-per-row (a second provenance pair per column was not worth the schema cost for a one-day build). The `faa_taf` row is always present in `source_vintage` and in `AirportProfile.vintages`, and neither column is ever a metric *value* — hub class is used only for peer grouping and filtering |
| 47 | The frozen contract suite was edited (2 assertions) so a real DuckDB implementation could pass it | Procedurally this needed a prior human decision and the implementer made it unilaterally; the human ratified it retroactively after reviewing the diff | The edit narrows nothing: `top_n=10_000` tests the same truncation semantics against any real route count, and the factory test now asserts the append mechanism instead of a closed set. The process breach is recorded here rather than quietly fixed |

## Out of scope / with more time

Ranked roughly by value per hour of work.

1. **The cut data sources.** BTS DB1B/OD-40 (`od_share` — the single strongest market-quality signal), BTS Delay
   Cause (`nas_delay_share` and a genuine 10-year delay trend), FAA CATS Form 127 (`cpe_usd`,
   `nonaero_rev_per_enpl`, and with them a real P5), FAA AIP grant histories (`aip_per_enpl_10y`), Census-BEA MSA
   GDP (`msa_gdp_per_capita`, `msa_gdp_cagr_5y`). Each is one adapter plus one derived function; the registry
   entries, the presets and the missing-reason plumbing are already in place.
2. **Gate curation.** Even 30 hand-curated, individually sourced gate counts would activate `pax_per_gate` and
   `deps_per_gate_day` and make `terminal_expansion` behave as designed.
3. **A longer OTP and T-100 window.** 36 months of OTP and 60 of T-100 would make the 3y and 5y congestion and
   market horizons real rather than None or partial-window.
4. **Sensitivity analysis.** Top-10 rank stability across presets and horizons, as design 02 intended — a small
   harness over `Analyst.rank` plus a table in this documentation set.
5. **LLM provider fail-over.** Groq and NVIDIA NIM as fallbacks behind the same LiteLLM router: config plus keys,
   no code change. That would remove the single point of failure that today is a loud 429.
6. **Voice.** `st.audio_input` -> speech-to-text through the same provider -> the existing text pipeline, with
   optional TTS of the headline. Designed (design 04) and dropped in the rescope; the assignment lists it as a
   bonus.
7. **International route detail.** Either the T-100 International Segment table code (a longer probe or a manual
   download) or a paid schedule feed, to replace airport-level international totals with real route-level data.
8. **Hosting and multi-user.** The app ships as a local Streamlit process with a throwaway key; a deployed
   instance needs authentication, per-user sessions and server-side key management.
9. **An MCP wrapper.** The same `ToolRegistry` exposed over FastMCP so other clients can call the deterministic
   engine directly.
10. **Richer presentation.** Charts beyond tables, and the 10-year enplanement sparkline in airport profiles that
    was designed as a nice-to-have and cut.
